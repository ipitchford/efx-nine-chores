# Source-to-claim audit — 8 October 2026

This was a retrospective publication-intake check, not a reconstructed discovery-time search. Queries included exact variants of EFX, nine chores, three agents and minimum lowering. It is bounded, not proof of priority.

* Zhang, arXiv:2609.10585v2: Theorem 1.2, §4 and §6 read. Same nonnegative additive model and all-owned-deletions convention; ordinary eight-chore existence supplies the shared-cheapest reduction. Section 6 leaves nine chores open. Its computational basis is not represented here as a formal theorem-prover verification.
* Kobayashi–Mahara–Sakamoto, arXiv:2305.04168, full-preprint Lemma 4.2 and its two-case proof read with the preceding EFX-graph definitions. Although placed in an identically ordered section, the proof only needs the inserted chore cheapest for all but one agent. The manuscript supplies that argument directly for three agents. Theorem 3.1 is the optional smaller-case baseline.
* He–Tao, arXiv:2606.08872: negative context for at least four agents, not three.
* Alkassar–Fouz–Mehlhorn, arXiv:2608.08590: computational additive-goods comparator, not the same chore statement.
* Akrami et al., arXiv:2604.18216v3 §9: abstract encoding validation in Lean, not verification of all implementation code. It is a methodological comparator for nonadditive goods.
* von zur Gathen–Sieveking (1978), DOI 10.1090/S0002-9939-1978-0500555-0: classical determinant/integer-bound context. No claim that the present specialization has established historical priority.

The written genericity, minimum-lowering, normalization, empty-bundle and literal-pruning arguments were examined independently of the solver verdict. A current semantic reconstruction checks the actual serialized input; see publication evidence. The stronger unrestricted chore theorem, incentive properties, empirical welfare effects and an absolute novelty claim are excluded.
